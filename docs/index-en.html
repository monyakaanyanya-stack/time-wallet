Why I made this

A year went by in an instant

I wanted to work, and kept finding myself scrolling instead. I hated that I couldn't focus. So I built something for myself: a way to start my own time before opening social media.

What I realised later was that it wasn't only work time I'd been losing. The year I spent with less aimless scrolling was astonishingly full. The years before it, I think I was just drifting. Between the time I kept watching without purpose and now, when I choose where my time goes, the number of days I actually remember is completely different.

I added the photo feature because I wanted to keep those remembered days. I won't tell you that you have to quit. If you don't want to, that's fine. But if you'd rather give your time to what you think is good, instead of what happens to scroll past — I'd like to help with that.

— From the one developer who made Yutora

How is this different from a normal app blocker?

It doesn't end at locking apps. Social media lives inside a daily budget, the time you spend on yourself is measured, and the day itself is kept in a photo. What remains isn't a record of what you quit — it's a record of what you lived.

Does it block social media completely?

No. You decide how much time you get each day. Only when you go past it are you gently pointed back to your own time. It isn't here to stop you posting, messaging or working. And it doesn't care whether you're on a screen or off it — the night you watched a film to the end, the day you finished a book: if you chose to give it your attention, that is your time.

Where are my photos and records stored?

On your device and in your own iCloud, and nowhere else. Nothing is sent to the developer's servers. Exporting and sharing are always your choice.

Can I keep videos?

Photos only — there is no video recording. What stays is a single still image. A photo takes one press of the shutter; a video means holding your phone up for as long as it runs. What this app wants to add to your day isn't time spent holding a screen — it's time spent looking at something with your own eyes. And once pictures start moving inside a grid, that grid is a feed.

How much can I do for free?

Photos, notes, recording your own time, monthly collages and keeping a day together all work without a subscription. Blocking starts with the one app you spend the most time in. Premium adds protection across several apps at once and looking back over any period. The yearly plan includes 14 days free. If you cancel, the records and photos you already have are not deleted.
